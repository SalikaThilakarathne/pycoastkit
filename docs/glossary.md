# Glossary

Coastal terms used in this documentation. Where a term is treated in
Roelvink and Reniers (2012), *A Guide to Modeling Coastal Morphology*
([full reference](citing.md#coastal-science)), the relevant section and page
are given as "RR §section, p. page" for further reading.

## Waves

**Significant wave height ($H_s$)**
:   A statistical measure of the height of irregular waves. In the time
    domain it is the mean height of the highest third of the waves
    ($H_{1/3}$); in the frequency domain it is $H_{m0} = 4\sqrt{m_0}$, where
    $m_0$ is the variance of the sea surface. Storm definitions in
    `pycoastkit` are based on $H_s$.

**Wave spectrum**
:   The distribution of wave energy over frequency (and direction),
    obtained from a record of the sea surface with a Fourier transform.
    Standard shapes include the Pierson-Moskowitz spectrum for fully
    developed seas and the more peaked JONSWAP spectrum for growing seas.
    RR §2.3, pp. 5–7.

**Sea and swell**
:   Sea is generated by the local wind and is steep, with broad frequency
    and directional spreading. Swell has travelled from distant storms and is
    longer, milder and narrow-banded. Their effects on the beach differ:
    swell becomes strongly skewed before breaking and tends to move sand
    onshore. RR §2.4, p. 8.

**Dispersion relation**
:   The relation $\sigma^2 = g k \tanh(kh)$ between wave frequency, wavelength
    and water depth from linear wave theory. `pck.wavenumber` solves it for
    $k$. RR §2.3, eq. 2.2, p. 6.

**Group velocity ($c_g$)**
:   The speed at which wave energy travels. It equals half the phase speed in
    deep water and the full phase speed in shallow water.

**Wave energy flux (wave power)**
:   The rate at which wave energy crosses a line parallel to the crests,
    $E c_g$ per metre of crest. As waves approach the shore, changes in group
    velocity and direction change the wave height (shoaling and refraction).
    RR §2.6.2, pp. 12–13. See `pck.wave_energy_flux`.

**Wave breaking (depth-limited)**
:   In shallow water, waves break when their height becomes too large for the
    local depth. Breaking transfers wave energy and momentum to currents,
    long waves and turbulence, and is the main driver of nearshore sediment
    transport. RR §2.2, p. 5.

**Infragravity waves**
:   Long waves with periods of roughly 25 s to a few minutes, generated by
    groups of short waves. They persist after the short waves have broken and
    dominate the swash during storms. RR §3.6, p. 62; §5.3.3, p. 121.

## Water levels and currents

**Mean high water springs (MHWS) and mean sea level (MSL)**
:   Reference tidal levels. At Hasaki, on the Hasaki Port datum, MHWS is
    1.25 m and MSL is 0.65 m; the shoreline in `pycoastkit` is usually taken
    at MHWS.

**Storm surge**
:   The rise of the water level above the predicted astronomical tide, caused
    by wind stress and low atmospheric pressure. Wind-driven set-up against
    the coast is one of its components. RR §3.4.2, p. 53. See
    `pck.storm_surge`.

**Wave set-up**
:   The rise of the mean water level inside the surf zone caused by breaking
    waves. RR §3.5.2, p. 54.

**Longshore current**
:   A current parallel to the shore, driven by obliquely breaking waves and
    by wind. Gradients in the resulting longshore sand transport cause
    long-term erosion or accretion. RR §3.5.1, p. 53; §5.3, p. 115.

**Return flow (undertow)**
:   The offshore-directed flow near the bed in the surf zone that balances the
    onshore mass flux of breaking waves. It is the dominant cause of offshore
    sand transport during storms, and so of dune erosion and offshore bar
    migration. RR §4.5, p. 107.

## Beach and nearshore morphology

**Cross-shore profile**
:   The elevation of the beach and seabed along a line perpendicular to the
    shore. Repeated surveys of a profile are the basic data of
    `pck.beach`.

**Equilibrium profile**
:   The characteristic profile shape a beach tends towards under steady
    conditions, often described as $h = A x^{2/3}$ (Dean, 1973). Because wave
    conditions change faster than the beach can respond, a real profile is
    never exactly in equilibrium. RR §5.3, p. 115.

**Shoreline**
:   The cross-shore position where the profile crosses a chosen water level,
    for example MHWS. See `pck.shoreline_positions`.

**Foreshore**
:   The part of the beach between low and high water, exposed to the swash.
    `pck.foreshore_slope` measures its mean slope between two water levels.

**Surf zone**
:   The nearshore area where waves break, between the outer breaker line and
    the shoreline.

**Swash zone and run-up**
:   The swash zone is the part of the beach that is alternately covered and
    exposed by waves running up and down the slope. Run-up is the highest
    level reached by the swash. RR §5.3.3, p. 121.

**Sandbar, crest and trough**
:   A sandbar is a shore-parallel ridge of sand in the surf zone; its highest
    point is the crest and the deeper channels on either side are troughs.
    Bars make large waves break offshore and protect the beach. Bars tend to
    move offshore during energetic, erosive conditions and onshore during
    moderate conditions. RR §5.3.1, p. 115. See `pck.sandbars`.

**Inner and outer bar**
:   Bars closer to and further from the shoreline. At Hasaki the boundary is
    taken at 170 m from the shoreline.

**Net offshore bar migration**
:   The long-term cycle on many barred coasts in which bars form near the
    shoreline, grow while moving offshore, and decay at the outer edge of the
    surf zone. Cycle times range from about 2 years on steeper beaches to
    about 12 years on gently sloping ones. RR §5.3.2, pp. 118–119.

**Depth of closure and active profile**
:   The depth beyond which profile changes become negligible over the time
    scale considered. The active profile extends from the dune top down to
    the depth of closure. RR §5.3.5, p. 126; §8.1, p. 179.

## Storms and beach response

**Storm**
:   In `pycoastkit`, a period in which $H_s$ stays above a threshold for a
    minimum duration and peaks above a higher threshold. See
    [Storms](guide/storms.md).

**Storm cluster**
:   A group of storms separated by calm spells too short for the beach to
    recover. The combined erosion of a cluster is often larger than that of
    a single storm of similar intensity.

**Storm power**
:   An index of storm intensity, $H_{s,\max}^2 \times$ duration (Dolan and
    Davis, 1992).

**Beach erosion and accretion**
:   The loss and gain of sand on the beach, measured as shoreline retreat and
    advance, or as volume change per metre of coast.

**Storm impact regimes**
:   The sequence of increasing impact on a dune or barrier coast: swash
    (erosion of the beach face), collision (waves attack the dune and it
    erodes), overwash (water flows over the dune crest) and inundation
    (the dune or barrier is submerged) (Sallenger, 2000). RR §5.3.3, p. 120.

**Dune erosion**
:   Retreat of the dune face during storms, when swash and infragravity waves
    reach the dune foot, the dune slumps, and the return flow carries the sand
    offshore. RR §5.3.3, pp. 120–122.

**Overwash**
:   Flow of water and sand over the dune or barrier crest, which deposits
    sand landward as overwash fans. RR §5.3.3, p. 122.

**Beach recovery**
:   The return of sand to the beach under calmer conditions after a storm,
    largely through onshore bar migration and onshore transport by
    asymmetric waves. RR §5.3, p. 115; §7.2, p. 173. See `pck.storm_recovery`.

**Beach vulnerability**
:   The degree to which a beach is eroded by storms. `pycoastkit` scores the
    observed impact with the beach vulnerability index and the pre-storm
    condition with a susceptibility index. See
    [Vulnerability](guide/vulnerability.md).
